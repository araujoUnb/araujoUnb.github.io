The same as for the linear modulations: signal, noise and SNR at point 1, before detection, and at point 2, after detection, always compared with \(\gamma = S_i/(N_0W)\). Main reference: J. G. Proakis and M. Salehi, Fundamentals of Communication Systems, ch. 6.

Angle modulation receiver
The message is in the phase. The FM receiver and the SNR before detection.

Where does the FM advantage come from?

press → to advance
In linear modulations, the best result with coherent detection is \(\gamma\), the same as baseband.
There the message is in the amplitude, and the noise adds directly to the amplitude.
In FM and PM the message is in the phase. The noise must first rotate the carrier phase.
A strong carrier rotates little: the larger \(A_c\), the smaller the phase noise.
The price is bandwidth: \(B_T = 2(\beta + 1)W\) by Carson's rule.

The same plan as for the linear modulations: signal, noise and SNR at point 1, before detection, and at point 2, after detection, always compared with \(\gamma = S_i/(N_0W)\).

FM receiver

One block per step · press → to advance
\[ s(t) = A_c\cos[2\pi f_c t + \phi(t)], \qquad \phi(t) = 2\pi k_f \int_{-\infty}^{t} m(\alpha)\,d\alpha \tag{1.1}\]
The FM signal has constant amplitude \(A_c\); the message is entirely in the phase \(\phi(t)\).
The bandpass filter passes the whole FM signal and cuts the noise outside \(f_c \pm B_T/2\).
The limiter fixes the amplitude: noise that only affects the envelope vanishes here.
The discriminator delivers the instantaneous frequency, the derivative of the phase divided by \(2\pi\).
The lowpass filter cuts everything above the message bandwidth \(W\).
In PM the detector delivers the phase \(\theta(t)\) itself: it is the discriminator followed by an integrator.
Same conditions as before: received power \(S_i\) and white noise with PSD \(N_0/2\).

Noise in the phase
The limiter erases the envelope. What reaches the detector is the phase of the sum of signal and noise.

Noise as envelope and phase

Bandpass noise with bandwidth \(B_T\) · press → to advance
Bandpass noise in phase and quadrature, now with bandwidth \(B_T\):
\[ n(t) = n_c(t)\cos(2\pi f_c t) - n_s(t)\sin(2\pi f_c t) \tag{2.1}\]
The same two terms written as a single sinusoid:
\[ n(t) = \cssId{ef-rn}{r_n(t)}\cos[2\pi f_c t + \cssId{ef-psi}{\psi_n(t)}] \tag{2.2}\]
Expanding the cosine of the sum, \(n_c = r_n\cos\psi_n\) and \(n_s = r_n\sin\psi_n\).
Since \(n_c\) and \(n_s\) are independent Gaussians with the same variance, the phase \(\psi_n\) is uniform on \([0, 2\pi)\): the noise has no preferred direction.
\(r_n = \sqrt{n_c^2 + n_s^2}\)
\(\psi_n = \arctan\dfrac{n_s}{n_c}\)

Signal plus noise: phasor diagram

Angles measured from the signal phase · press → to advance
At the limiter input:
\[ r(t) = A_c\cos[2\pi f_c t + \phi] + r_n\cos[2\pi f_c t + \psi_n] \tag{2.3}\]
The sum is a single sinusoid
\[ r(t) = \cssId{fs-R}{R(t)}\cos[2\pi f_c t + \theta(t)] \tag{2.4}\]
Angle from the legs of the triangle
\[ \theta = \phi + \arctan\frac{r_n\sin(\psi_n - \phi)}{A_c + r_n\cos(\psi_n - \phi)} \tag{2.5}\]
Only the phase \(\theta(t)\) reaches the detector. The term after \(\phi\) is the phase noise.
the limiter erases it

FM, point 2: the discriminator differentiates

press → to advance
In FM, \(\phi(t) = 2\pi k_f \int m\,d\alpha\), and the discriminator delivers the instantaneous frequency:
Discriminator
\[ y(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{dt} \]
\[ = \frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{dt} + \frac{1}{2\pi A_c}\frac{dn_s}{dt} \]

Trading bandwidth for SNR

FM with a tone versus the linear modulations · press → to advance
Carson: \(B_T/W = 2(\beta + 1)\), so
\[ \frac{\text{SNR}}{\gamma} = \frac32\beta^2, \quad \beta = \frac{B_T}{2W} - 1 \tag{4.7}\]
For large \(\beta\), \(\text{SNR} \propto (B_T/W)^2\): doubling the bandwidth gains 6 dB.
FM beats the linear modulations only when \(\tfrac32\beta^2 \gt 1\), that is \(\beta \gt 0.82\) and \(B_T \gt 3.6\,W\).
All this holds above threshold, with \(A_c \gg r_n\). Below it, the \(\arctan\) approximation fails and FM degrades abruptly.

Threshold effect
With strong noise the phasor encircles the origin, the phase jumps by \(2\pi\) and the loudspeaker pops.

Strong noise: the phasor encircles the origin

press → to advance
Above threshold, \(r_n \ll A_c\) almost all the time: the tip of \(R\) stays near the end of \(A_c\) and \(\theta - \phi\) only oscillates around zero.
When the input SNR drops, \(r_n\) reaches the order of \(A_c\) and the tip of \(R\) can pass to the other side of the origin.
If it encircles the origin, \(\theta\) gains or loses \(2\pi\) within a short interval.
If the tip goes out and back without encircling the origin, the phase returns to its initial value and nothing remains. Only a full turn leaves a mark.

A full turn becomes a click

press → to advance
Area of the pulse at the discriminator output:
\[ \int y\,dt = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int \frac{d\theta}{dt}\,dt \]
Integral of the derivative
\[ = \frac{1}{2\pi}\,\cssId{cq-dt}{\bigl[\theta(t_2) - \theta(t_1)\bigr]} \]
\[ = \cg{\pm 1} \tag{5.1}\]
The pulse lasts about \(1/B_T\), much less than \(1/W\). For the lowpass filter of bandwidth \(W\), it is an impulse:
\[ \pm\,\delta(t - t_k) \tag{5.2}\]
In the loudspeaker, each impulse sounds like a pop: the click.
a full turn: \(\pm 2\pi\)

Threshold: SNR at the knee

press → to advance

6
Pre-emphasis and de-emphasis
Boost the high frequencies before modulating and attenuate them after demodulating: the signal comes out the same and the high-frequency noise drops.

FM noise lives at high frequencies

press → to advance
The noise PSD after the discriminator grows with \(f^2\): almost nothing near \(f = 0\) and the maximum at \(f = W\).
Audio does the opposite: the energy of voice and music is at low frequencies and falls above about 2 kHz.
At high frequencies the signal is weak exactly where the noise is strong.
The idea: boost the high frequencies of the message before modulating and attenuate them back after demodulating. The signal comes out the same; the high-frequency noise is attenuated.

The two filters

Pre-emphasis at the transmitter, de-emphasis at the receiver · press → to advance
Pre-emphasis, a gentle highpass filter:
\[ H_{pe}(f) = 1 + j\,\frac{f}{f_0} \tag{6.1}\]
De-emphasis, the inverse, an \(RC\) lowpass filter:
\[ H_{de}(f) = \frac{1}{1 + j f/f_0} \tag{6.2}\]
The message goes through both and comes out intact.
\[ H_{pe}(f)\,H_{de}(f) = \cg{1} \]
The noise enters after the pre-emphasis: it only goes through the de-emphasis.
FM radio: time constant \(\tau = RC = 75\,\mu\)s.
\[ f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\tau} \]
\[ f_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot 75\times 10^{-6}} \]
\[ f_0 = \cb{2.1\ \text{kHz}} \tag{6.3}\]
Below \(f_0\) the filters barely change anything; above it, the pre-emphasis rises 6 dB per octave.

Noise after de-emphasis

press → to advance
Without de-emphasis, the noise PSD is the parabola, (4.2):
\[ S_n(f) = \frac{N_0}{A_c^2}\,f^2 \]
De-emphasis multiplies the PSD by \(|H_{de}(f)|^2\):
\[ |H_{de}(f)|^2 = \cssId{pe-hd}{\frac{1}{1 + (f/f_0)^2}} \tag{6.4}\]
Product
\[ S_{de}(f) = \frac{N_0}{A_c^2}\,\frac{f^2}{1 + (f/f_0)^2} \tag{6.5}\]
\(|1 + jx|^2 = 1 + x^2\)

Noise power with de-emphasis

Preparing the integrand · press → to advance
The lowpass filter of bandwidth \(W\) integrates the de-emphasized PSD:
\[ N_{de} = \frac{N_0}{A_c^2}\int_{-W}^{W} \cssId{pd-ig}{\frac{f^2}{1 + (f/f_0)^2}}\,df \tag{6.6}\]
Factoring out \(f_0^2\), and adding and subtracting 1 in the numerator:
\[ \frac{f^2}{1 + (f/f_0)^2} = f_0^2\,\frac{\cssId{pd-nm}{(f/f_0)^2 + 1 - 1}}{1 + (f/f_0)^2} \]
Split the fraction
\[ \frac{f^2}{1 + (f/f_0)^2} = f_0^2\Bigl[\,\cssId{pd-um}{1} - \cssId{pd-fr}{\frac{1}{1 + (f/f_0)^2}}\,\Bigr] \tag{6.7}\]

Solving the integral

press → to advance
(6.7) inside (6.6)
\[ \int_{-W}^{W} \frac{f^2}{1 + (f/f_0)^2}\,df = \int_{-W}^{W} f_0^2\,df - f_0^2\int_{-W}^{W}\frac{df}{1 + (f/f_0)^2} \]
First part: \(\displaystyle\int_{-W}^{W} f_0^2\,df = 2f_0^2W\).
Second part, with \(u = f/f_0\) and \(df = f_0\,du\):
\[ f_0^2 \cdot f_0\cssId{in-at}{\int_{-W/f_0}^{W/f_0}\frac{du}{1 + u^2}} \]
Putting it together:
\[ 2f_0^2W - 2f_0^3\arctan\frac{W}{f_0} \]
Factoring out \(2f_0^3\), with \(r = W/f_0\):
\[ = 2f_0^3\,(r - \arctan r) \]
Substitute into (6.6)
\[ \boxed{N_{de} = \frac{2N_0 f_0^3}{A_c^2}\,(r - \arctan r)} \tag{6.8}\]
\(= 2\arctan(W/f_0)\)

Gain of pre-emphasis and de-emphasis

press → to advance
The signal comes out the same, so the SNR gain is the ratio of the noises:
\[ G = \frac{N_o}{N_{de}} \]
\[ = \frac{\cssId{ga-no}{2N_0W^3/(3A_c^2)}}{2N_0f_0^3\,(r - \arctan r)/A_c^2} \]
Canceling \(2N_0/A_c^2\) and using \(W^3/f_0^3 = r^3\):
\[ \boxed{G = \frac{r^3/3}{r - \arctan r}}, \qquad r = \frac{W}{f_0} \tag{6.9}\]
For large \(r\), \(\arctan r \to \pi/2\) is small compared with \(r\):
\[ G \approx \frac{r^2}{3} \tag{6.10}\]
The gain grows with the square of \(W/f_0\).
The price: pre-emphasis boosts the high frequencies and can increase the peak deviation. Since audio has little energy above \(f_0\), the deviation barely changes and \(\beta\) stays the same.
\(N_o\): the whole parabola, (4.3)
