Why communications?
Everything around us is communication. Signals, spectrum and modulation are the language of the modern world.

We are surrounded by communications

Everyday examples · press → to advance
AM/FM radio: amplitude and frequency modulation
4G/5G cell phones: OFDM, MIMO
Wi-Fi: IEEE 802.11, multiple carriers
GPS: CDMA, pseudorandom code
Digital TV: ISDB-T, FEC
Optical fiber: WDM
What do they all have in common?
Signals, spectrum and modulation!

The communication system: overview

From source to destination · press → to advance
The information source produces the message \(m(t)\).
The transmitter performs the modulation: the message \(m(t)\) becomes the transmitted signal \(s(t)\).
The channel receives \(s(t)\) and also the noise \(n(t)\); out of it comes the received signal \(r(t)\).
The receiver performs the demodulation and delivers the estimate \(\hat{m}(t)\) of the message to the destination.
How do we represent the message as a signal?
How do we transmit efficiently over the channel?
How do we fight noise and distortion?
What is the theoretical limit on performance?

The three fundamental challenges

Every communication system faces three questions · press → to advance
1. Spectrum

What bandwidth \(B\) does the signal occupy?

AM radio: 10 kHz · FM radio: 200 kHz · TV: 6 MHz

2. Noise

How does noise degrade the received signal?

\[ \text{SNR} = \frac{\cssId{d-ps}{P_{\text{signal}}}}{\cssId{d-pn}{P_{\text{noise}}}} \tag{1.1}\]

The higher, the better!

3. Modulation

How do we adapt the signal to the channel?

AM, FM, PM: each with its own advantages

SNR of the middle panel: 10 dB
This course teaches how to analyze and design systems that solve these challenges.
average power of the useful signal
average noise power in the same band

Evolution of communication systems

From Marconi to 5G · press → to advance
1895: Marconi's radio. The era of analog modulation begins.
1920: AM radio.
1933: FM, by Armstrong.
1948: Shannon publishes the theoretical foundation of communications.
1960s: satellite TV.
1991: GSM, the digital cell phone.
2020s: 5G and beyond, with digital modulation.

The electromagnetic spectrum in communications

Bands and applications · press → to advance
From the LF/MF band, starting at 30 kHz, up to optical, in the THz range.
AM radio in LF/MF.
FM radio in VHF.
Wi-Fi and cell phones in UHF/SHF.
GPS and satellite, also in UHF/SHF.
5G in millimeter waves, from 30 to 300 GHz.
Optical fiber, in the optical band.
In this course, we will learn to analyze signals and systems in any frequency band. The same principles, Fourier, modulation and filtering, apply from AM radio to 5G.

AM and FM radio: the classic that still works

Press → to advance
AM radio, from 540 to 1600 kHz
\[ s(t) = A_c\big[1 + \cssId{am-mu}{\mu\, m(t)}\big]\cos(2\pi f_c t) \tag{3.1}\]
Simple demodulation: envelope detector.
Long range; problem: susceptible to noise.
FM radio, from 88 to 108 MHz: the frequency varies
\[ f_i(t) = f_c + \cssId{fm-kf}{k_f\, m(t)} \tag{3.2}\]
Carson's rule
\[ B \approx 2\big(\cssId{fm-df}{\Delta f} + \cssId{fm-fm}{f_m}\big) \tag{3.3}\]
Advantage: high immunity to noise.
Better audio quality, stereo.
\(A_c,\ f_c\)
carrier amplitude and frequency
\(m(t),\ \mu\)
message; AM modulation index
\(f_i(t),\ k_f\)
instantaneous frequency; FM modulator sensitivity
\(\Delta f,\ f_m\)
peak frequency deviation; message frequency
the envelope follows \(m(t)\)
the message shifts the frequency
\(\Delta f = k_f \max|m(t)|\)

\[ N_T = \frac{\cssId{nt-p1}{P_1} + \cssId{nt-p2}{2P_2} + \cssId{nt-p3}{3P_3}}{\cssId{nt-6}{6}} \tag{4.1}\]

Lab grade \(N_L\), one report per lab session
\[ N_L = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{8} \text{Rep}_k}{\cssId{nl-8}{8}} \tag{4.2}\]
Final grade \(N_F\)
\[ N_F = \cssId{nf-75}{0.75}\,N_T + \cssId{nf-25}{0.25}\,N_L \tag{4.3}\]
\(P_1,\ P_2,\ P_3\)
exams of Modules 1, 2 and 3, with weights 1, 2 and 3
\(\text{Rep}_k\)
grade of the report for lab session \(k\)
the denominator is the sum of the weights
8 lab sessions, all with the same weight
\(0.75 + 0.25 = 1\): theory 75%, lab 25%

A Conda-based distribution with GNU Radio and SDR libraries ready to use
