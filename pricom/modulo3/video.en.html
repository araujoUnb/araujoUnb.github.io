Rate and color
How many bits an uncompressed video has, and how color perception already saves half of the samples.

Uncompressed video

HDTV, 30 frames per second, 8 bits per sample · press →
Bit rate of raw video
\[ R=\cssId{tx-px}{1920\times1080}\times1.5\times8\times30 \]
Pixels times samples per pixel
\[ 2\,073\,600\times\cssId{tx-am}{1.5}=3\,110\,400\ \text{samples} \]
8 bits per sample, 30 frames per second
\[ 3\,110\,400\times8\times30\approx\cssId{tx-r}{746\ \text{Mbit/s}} \]
Two hours of film, 7200 s
\[ \frac{746\times10^{6}\times7200}{8}\approx670\ \text{GB} \]

\[ \frac{746}{19.39}\approx38\ \text{times less} \]
DPCM would give a factor of 2. The key is visual perception: the eye does not notice much of what is thrown away.

Chroma subsampling

Luma at every pixel, chroma at one in four · press →
Color is stored as Y′CbCr instead of RGB: Y′ is the brightness, the luma; Cb and Cr are the blue and red differences, the chroma.
The eye sees detail in brightness much better than in color. That is why the luma is sampled at every pixel.
In the 4:2:0 format of MPEG-2, every 8 luma samples come with only 2 Cb and 2 Cr: \[ \frac{8+2+2}{8}=1.5\ \text{samples per pixel} \] against 3 for full RGB. Color video has only 50 % more samples than black and white.
2
Motion
Neighboring frames are almost equal. The MPEG encoder is a DPCM in which the sample is a whole frame, with motion estimation.

Redundancy between frames

Fig. 5.36 of Lathi · press →
Each frame is divided into blocks. Here, a fixed scene and a person.
In the next frame the person has walked three blocks. The scene is the same.
Only 4 of the 48 blocks changed: the ones the person left and the ones the person now occupies. Sending only the difference exploits the temporal redundancy, between frames.
Within a single frame there is redundancy too: blocks of sky or wall have almost all pixels equal. This is the spatial redundancy, handled by the DCT.

MPEG encoder

Fig. 5.37 of Lathi, simplified · press →
Look at the structure: it is a DPCM in which the sample is a whole frame.
Forward path: the residual, frame minus prediction, goes through the DCT and the quantization block by block.
Loop: the encoder reconstructs the frame as the receiver will and stores it in memory. It is the same DPCM trick that keeps the error from accumulating.
The prediction is not just the previous frame: motion estimation shifts each block to where it went, and the vectors are also sent over the channel.

Transform and quantization
DCT of 8 × 8 blocks, quantization with a matrix, zigzag scan and reconstruction, on the block of Table 5.2 of Lathi.

Discrete cosine transform

Eq. 5.52a of Lathi · press →
DCT of an \(N\times N\) block
\[ \begin{gathered} F(\ell,k)=\frac{C(\ell)\,C(k)}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}f(n,m)\,\cssId{dc-c}{\cos\frac{(2n+1)\ell\pi}{2N}}\,\cos\frac{(2m+1)k\pi}{2N} \\ C(k)=\begin{cases}1, & k=0\\ \sqrt2, & k\ge1\end{cases} \end{gathered} \tag{5.52a}\]
Basis functions in one dimension, \(N=8\), at the top of the figure.
\(k=0\) is constant: \(F(0,0)\) is proportional to the block mean, the DC level. The others oscillate faster and faster.
In two dimensions, the basis function \((\ell,k)\) is the product of a cosine along the rows and one along the columns. A smooth block, as is almost always the case in an image, is written with a few low-frequency basis functions.
measures how much the block resembles a cosine with \(\ell\) half cycles

DCT coefficients

Table 5.3 of Lathi · press →
The 64 pixels became 64 coefficients: nothing has been lost yet, the IDCT returns exactly.
The energy concentrated in the top corner: low frequencies in both directions.
Outside the corner, almost everything has magnitude below 5, against 1240 for the DC.
These small coefficients can go to zero without the eye noticing. That is where compression happens.

Quantization of the coefficients

Tables 5.4 and 5.5 of Lathi · press →
Each coefficient has its own step size
\[ P_n(\ell,k)=\text{round}\,\frac{F(\ell,k)}{\cssId{qt-q}{Q(\ell,k)}} \tag{5.53}\]
Three calculations
\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{1240.5}{16}&=77.5\ \to\ 78\\[8pt] \frac{56.83}{12}&=4.74\ \to\ 5\\[8pt] \frac{\cssId{qt-f}{7.14}}{61}&=0.12\ \to\ 0 \end{aligned} \]
19 nonzero coefficients out of 64 remain, all in the top corner.
Multiplying \(Q\) by \(Q_p\) trades quality for rate: the compression knob
\[ P_n(\ell,k)=\text{round}\,\frac{F(\ell,k)}{Q_p\,Q(\ell,k)} \]
small step at low frequencies, large at high ones
\(F(0,7)\): less than half a step, becomes zero

Zigzag scan

Fig. 5.39 of Lathi · press →
The quantized matrix \(P_n\), Table 5.5, is still a grid of 64 numbers.
It is read along diagonals, from low to high frequencies.
78 1 5 −4 −1 2 −2 −1 2 3 −1 −1 −1 0 2 0 −1 1 1 0 1 0 0 0 −1
And then 39 zeros in a row, replaced by a single end-of-block symbol.
Pairs of how many zeros and which value receive variable-length codes
\[ 64\ \text{numbers}\ \to\ 25+\text{end of block} \]

Reconstruction at the decoder

Tables 5.6 and 5.7 of Lathi · press →
It knows \(P_n\) and \(Q\): undo the normalization, Table 5.6
\[ \begin{aligned} \hat F(\ell,k)&=P_n(\ell,k)\,Q(\ell,k)\\ \hat F(0,0)&=78\times16=1248\\ \hat F(1,0)&=5\times12=60 \end{aligned} \]
Apply the IDCT
\[ \begin{gathered} \hat f(n,m)=\frac1N\sum_{\ell}\sum_{k}C(\ell)C(k)\hat F(\ell,k)\\ \times\cos\tfrac{(2n+1)\ell\pi}{2N}\cos\tfrac{(2m+1)k\pi}{2N} \end{gathered} \tag{5.52b}\]
Table 5.7: the block comes back very similar.

\[ \begin{aligned} e_{\text{rms}}&=\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{64}\textstyle\sum\sum(\hat f-f)^2}=4.65\\[4pt] \text{PSNR}&=20\log_{10}\frac{255}{\cssId{rc-e}{4.65}}=20\log_{10}54.8=34.8\ \text{dB} \end{aligned} \]

Frames and standards
I, P and B frames, the transmission order and the MPEG and H.26x family of standards.

I, P and B frames

Group of pictures, GOP · press →
I: coded on its own, with DCT only, like a photo. It is where a viewer who turns on the TV midway gets in.
P: predicted from the previous I or P, with motion vectors and residual.
B: predicted from the past and the future. It is the cheapest, and most frames are B.
The P must arrive before the B frames that depend on it. The whole group of pictures is processed at once: delay.

  • The gain from one standard to the next comes from variable-size blocks, more prediction modes and better entropy coding.

Two calculations worked out on the board: the rate of a 4K video in the three color formats and the average rate of an IBBP group of frames.

Exercise 1: uncompressed 4K video

Samples per pixel in 4:4:4, 4:2:2 and 4:2:0 · press →
A 4K video has \(3840\times2160\)-pixel frames, \(30\) frames per second and \(10\) bits per sample, in Y′CbCr. How much does it take uncompressed in each color format? First, we count the samples in a group of \(8\) pixels.
4:4:4: chroma at every pixel
\[ \frac{8+\cssId{ea-c4}{8+8}}{8}=3\ \text{samples per pixel} \]
4:2:2: chroma every two pixels in a row
\[ \frac{8+\cssId{ea-c2}{4+4}}{8}=2\ \text{samples per pixel} \]
4:2:0: chroma once per 2 × 2 block
\[ \frac{8+\cssId{ea-c0}{2+2}}{8}=\cssId{ea-r0}{1.5}\ \text{samples per pixel} \]
Luma alone would give \(1\) sample per pixel. 4:2:0 has half the samples of 4:4:4 and only \(50\) % more than black-and-white video.
8 Cb and 8 Cr
one Cb and one Cr per pair
one Cb and one Cr per 2 × 2 block

Average bit rate · press →
Video of \(1920\times1080\) in 4:2:0, \(8\) bits and \(60\) frames per second. The group has \(12\) frames, IBBPBBPBBPBB. On average, an I takes \(900\) kbit, a P \(300\) kbit and a B \(90\) kbit. What is the average rate?
Count the types in the group
\[ \cssId{ga-n}{1\ \text{I}+3\ \text{P}+8\ \text{B}}=12\ \text{frames} \]
Bits of each type
\[ 1\times900+3\times300+8\times\cssId{ga-b}{90} \]
Group total
\[ =900+900+720=\cssId{ga-tot}{2520\ \text{kbit}} \]
Group duration
\[ \frac{12\ \text{frames}}{60\ \text{frames/s}}=\cssId{ga-t}{0.2\ \text{s}} \]
Average rate
\[ R=\frac{2520\ \text{kbit}}{0.2\ \text{s}}=\cssId{ga-r}{12.6\ \text{Mbit/s}} \]
