Ideal sampling
How often to sample, the ideal sampler as a product with an impulse train, and what this does to the spectrum.

How often should we sample?

Motivation · press → to advance
Two signals: \(x_1(t)\) varies slowly, \(x_2(t)\) has high frequency components.
Both are sampled with the same interval \(T_s\).
Joining the samples with straight lines, \(x_1(t)\) comes back almost unchanged. In \(x_2(t)\) the fast oscillations vanish between one sample and the next.
The smaller the bandwidth of the signal, the larger \(T_s\) can be. The sampling theorem states exactly what the largest \(T_s\) is and how to reconstruct the signal with no error at all.

\[ \delta_{T_s}(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}c_k\,e^{j2\pi k f_s t},\quad f_s=\frac{1}{T_s} \]

\[ c_k=\frac{1}{T_s}\int_{-T_s/2}^{T_s/2}\cssId{sf-dts}{\delta_{T_s}(t)}\,e^{-j2\pi k f_s t}\,dt \]

Fourier series from (3)
\[ \delta_{T_s}(t)=\frac{1}{T_s}\sum_{k}\cssId{te-exk}{e^{j2\pi k f_s t}} \]

\[ \Delta(f)=\frac{1}{T_s}\sum_{k}\cssId{te-dk}{\delta(f-kf_s)} \tag{4}\]

Sampling theorem
When the replicas of \(X(f)\) do not overlap, the signal can be recovered from the samples: hence the Nyquist rate.

When the replicas touch

Derivation · press → to advance
Large \(f_s\): between \(W\) and \(f_s-W\) an empty band is left. The \(k=0\) replica is intact.
\(f_s=2W\): the replicas touch, still without overlap.
\(f_s<2W\): the replicas add up. The central copy is distorted and no filter can separate it. This is aliasing.

Sampling theorem

Statement and Nyquist rate · press → to advance
Theorem

If \(X(f)=0\) for \(|f|>W\) and the signal is sampled with \(f_s=1/T_s\ge 2W\), then \(x(t)\) is recovered exactly from the samples \(x(nT_s)\).

  • Nyquist rate: the smallest rate with no aliasing, \(f_s=2W\).
  • Nyquist interval: the largest interval, \(T_s=\dfrac{1}{2W}\).
  • Telephone voice, \(W=3.4\) kHz: Nyquist rate of \(6.8\) kHz. In practice \(8\) kHz is used.
  • CD audio, \(W=20\) kHz: Nyquist rate of \(40\) kHz. In practice \(44.1\) kHz is used.
Why sample above the Nyquist rate? The answer lies in the filter that reconstructs the signal.

Interpolation formula

Derivation · press → to advance
In time, filtering is convolving
\[ x(t)=x_\delta(t)*h(t) \]
Use (2)
\[ x(t)=\Big[\sum_{n}x(nT_s)\,\delta(t-nT_s)\Big]*h(t) \]
Convolution term by term
\[ x(t)=\sum_{n}x(nT_s)\,\cssId{if-hn}{h(t-nT_s)} \]
Use (9) with \(t\to t-nT_s\)
\[ x(t)=\cssId{if-form}{\sum_{n}2W'T_s\,x(nT_s)\operatorname{sinc}\!\big(2W'(t-nT_s)\big)} \tag{10}\]
Each sample multiplies a copy of \(h(t)\) shifted to its own instant. Adding these copies recovers \(x(t)\).
\(\delta(t-t_0)*h(t)=h(t-t_0)\)
holds for any \(W\le W'\le f_s-W\)

Interpolation at the Nyquist rate

Derivation · press → to advance
At the Nyquist rate there is no slack between the replicas
\[ W\le W'\le f_s-W\quad\text{with}\quad f_s=2W \]
The two limits coincide
\[ W'=W=\frac{f_s}{2}=\frac{1}{2T_s} \]
Gain in (10)
\[ \cssId{ny-g}{2W'T_s} \]
\[ =2\cdot\frac{1}{2T_s}\cdot T_s=1 \]
Argument in (10)
\[ \cssId{ny-a}{2W'(t-nT_s)} \]
\[ =\frac{t-nT_s}{T_s}=\frac{t}{T_s}-n \]

Sinc interpolation

Eq. (11) drawn · press → to advance
Samples \(x(nT_s)\) of a signal of bandwidth \(W\), taken at the Nyquist rate.
Each sample becomes a sinc centered at its own instant. It equals \(x(2T_s)\) at \(t=2T_s\) and zero at all the other sampling instants.
One sinc for each sample, with the height of the sample.
The sum of the sincs is the continuous signal. At the instants \(nT_s\) only one sinc is nonzero, so the sum passes exactly through each sample.
Between the samples, all the sincs contribute. This is ideal interpolation, which the linear interpolation of the first example only approximates.

Guard band

Example · press → to advance
Telephony: voice limited to \(W=3.4\) kHz and sampled at \(f_s=8\) kHz, above the Nyquist rate.
Between \(W\) and \(f_s-W\) there is an empty band, the guard band \(W_G=f_s-2W\).
A real filter does not cut vertically. The guard band leaves room for the transition between the passband and the stopband.
Which \(f_s\) guarantees a guard band of \(1200\) Hz for \(W=3400\) Hz?
Solve \(W_G=f_s-2W\) for \(f_s\)
\[ f_s=\cssId{bg-nyq}{2W}+\cssId{bg-wg}{W_G} \]
\[ =2\times 3400+1200 \]
\[ =\boxed{8000\text{ Hz}} \tag{13}\]

Complete chain

Summary · press → to advance
  • A real signal is not strictly band-limited: the anti-aliasing filter zeroes \(X(f)\) above \(W\).
  • With \(f_s\ge 2W\) the replicas do not overlap.
  • The reconstruction filter separates the central replica, which is the spectrum of the original signal.
  • Time is now discrete, but each sample still has a continuous amplitude. The next step is quantization.
